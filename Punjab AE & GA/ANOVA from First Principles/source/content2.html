6.1Reading the summary table

"Total var" is SST/(n − 1), the variance you would compute ignoring groups. MSwithin is what remains after each group gets its own mean. The ratio between them is a quick sense-check. For the call-centre data the total variance of 0.48 shrinks to 0.03 once training type is known; for the batteries it barely changes ([[O['batt']['totvar']|.2f]] → [[O['batt']['msw']|.2f]]).

6.2How big is the effect? η² and ω²

$$\eta^2=\frac{\SSB}{\SST}\qquad \omega^2=\frac{\SSB-(k-1)\,\MSW}{\SST+\MSW}$$

η² is the share of the sample's SST associated with the grouping. It is biased upward: even pure noise produces some SSB. ω² subtracts the SSB we would expect from noise alone, so it estimates the population share more honestly. For fertilizer: η² = [[O['fert']['eta']|.3f]] but ω² = [[O['fert']['omega']|.3f]]. With only five observations per group the gap matters. For batteries η² = [[O['batt']['eta']|.3f]] while ω² is negative (reported as 0).

6.3Which groups differ? Post-hoc comparisons

A significant omnibus F says at least one mean differs. To find which, compare pairs while controlling the chance of any false alarm across all comparisons. Tukey's Honest Significant Difference (HSD) is the standard choice for all pairwise comparisons.

[[fig('f_tukey','Figure 7. Tukey 95% family-wise intervals for the fertilizer data. B beats both A and C by about 4–5 q/acre (orange intervals exclude zero). A and C cannot be told apart (grey interval straddles zero). This is the practical conclusion the F test alone could not give.')]]

6.4NEW Example 11: a null result (battery life)

Three brands, five batteries each: means [[O['batt']['means'][0]|.2f]], [[O['batt']['means'][1]|.2f]] and [[O['batt']['means'][2]|.2f]] hours. F = [[O['batt']['F']|.2f]], p = [[O['batt']['p']|.2f]]. F is below 1, which is exactly what equal means would typically produce.

[[anova_table('batt','brands')]] [[call('warn','Absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence','alert')]]

It is tempting to write "the brands are identical". But with n = 5 per brand, a 95% interval for any pairwise difference is roughly ±[[2.179*(2*O['batt']['msw']/5)**0.5|.2f]] hours wide. The honest statement is: "We found no evidence of a difference; the data are consistent with differences of up to about [[2.179*(2*O['batt']['msw']/5)**0.5|.1f]] hours either way." Report the interval, not just the p-value.

6.5NEW Example 12: unequal spreads (courier delivery time)

The Fast and Local couriers are extremely consistent (variances [[O['courier']['vars'][0]|.1f]] and [[O['courier']['vars'][2]|.1f]]), while Budget is erratic (variance [[O['courier']['vars'][1]|.1f]]). Classical ANOVA pools all three variances into one MSwithin = [[O['courier']['msw']|.1f]], which is dominated by Budget. That inflated "noise" swamps the clear 5-hour gap between Fast ([[O['courier']['means'][0]|.1f]] h) and Local ([[O['courier']['means'][2]|.1f]] h).

When group variances differ a lot, and especially when group sizes are unequal too, use Welch's ANOVA (oneway.test() in R). Many statisticians now use it by default: when the variances really are equal, it loses almost nothing.

7ANOVA inside regression: farm yield

Model: yield per acre ~ farm size + fertilizer. Both predictors are continuous, so this is multiple linear regression. But OLS regression has the same decomposition: $\SST=\text{SSR}+\text{SSE}$ (model + residual). The overall regression F test is an ANOVA F test comparing the model with an intercept-only model.

The overall F is huge (p ≈ 4 × 10⁻⁷), yet neither slope is individually significant (p ≈ 0.51 each). Alone, each predictor is highly significant (R² ≈ 0.96, p ≈ 2.5 × 10⁻⁸). The reason is collinearity: the variance inflation factor is VIF = 1/(1 − r²) ≈ [[reg['vif']|.0f]], so each slope's variance is inflated about [[reg['vif']|.0f]]-fold. The coefficients "0.119 per acre" and "0.054 per kg" are not interpretable separately here. To separate them you would need farms where size and fertilizer vary independently, ideally by randomising fertilizer.

The previous edition printed 18.501 + 0.117·size + 0.054·fertilizer and F = 114.25. Recomputed from the printed data, the values are [[reg['b'][0]|.3f]], [[reg['b'][1]|.3f]], [[reg['b'][2]|.4f]] and F = [[reg['F']|.2f]]. The original was probably fitted to unrounded yields.

7.1A warning about causal interpretation

Regression ANOVA shows whether the predictors jointly explain variation beyond an intercept-only model. It does not, by itself, show that farm size or fertilizer causes yield changes. Causal claims need design: random assignment, or credible control of confounding. Each coefficient answers a partial question: the expected change in yield with one predictor, holding the other fixed.

8Two-way ANOVA and interactions

With two factors, the model part of the variation can be split further: into the main effect of factor A, the main effect of factor B, their interaction A × B, and the residual. An interaction means the effect of one factor depends on the level of the other.

Toy design: 3 seed varieties × 3 irrigation levels × 3 replicate plots (27 plots). To keep the data fully reproducible, the three replicates in every cell are cell mean − 1, cell mean, cell mean + 1. So the within-cell SS is always 9 × 2 = 18 on 18 df, and MSresidual = 1. We compare two scenarios with different cell means.

In Scenario 2 the interaction is huge (F = [[tw_int['rows'][2]['F']|.1f]]). The variety main effect of [[tw_int['rows'][0]['ss']|.1f]] averages over opposite patterns and should not be interpreted on its own: always look at the interaction first. Notice that the residual MS is 1.00 in both scenarios, because the noise is identical. What changes is only how the model variation is divided up.
